Eyes on the robot, without the robot’s software

A 910 KB static binary talks straight to /dev/spidev0.0 and /dev/gpiochip0, wakes both panels and plays a heart that grows from small to large, while it keeps three watchdogs alive.

128 × 64pixels per eye
1 MHzSPI clock
3watchdogs fed
12heart frames

Wiring

Each eye has its own chip select. Data/command and reset lines are shared, so the init sequence goes to both eyes.

On pre-3.0 boards DC is line 79 and RESET is line 216, so the binary then leaves 216 alone instead of toggling it.

Waking the controller

Ten command bytes, in this exact order, each sent with DC low. Then 0xAF turns the display on.

start00
horizontal addressing2000
pages 0 to 7220007
command + value81FF
command + value82FF
display onAF

Filled bytes are commands, outlined bytes are their parameters. A “cleaned up” 8-byte version that drops the second 00 or swaps 22 for 27 only lights a faint band of pixels.

Stored frame to wire stream

You draw in normal coordinates. The controller wants eight vertical pixels per byte, with pages and columns reversed, so stored_to_wire() flips the picture 180° before sending. Measured on the robot: the panel shows the stored frame 1:1.

stored: bit = y·128 + x, MSB first

wire: page = 7 − (y>>3), column = 127 − x

Three watchdogs, one fast deadline

Stopping every feeder resets the robot in about 1 to 2 seconds, not 10. The 1 s watchdog sets the pace, and the kernel cannot be told to stop it.

/dev/watchdog0 · timeout 1 s
/dev/watchdog1 · timeout 120 s
gpio 216 · external board
Feeding every 250 ms: all bars stay full.
Reset. watchdog0 ran out. In real life the board reboots and the stock stack brings the eyes back.
One feeder, one process. The watchdog devices allow a single open at a time. A second toadi_eyes gets EBUSY forever, so the binary refuses to start twice.

What the binary does at startup

  1. 1Detect the board from the EEPROM: V3 uses pins 2 and 0, V2 uses 79 and 216.
  2. 2Start the watchdog feeders first: both devices and gpio 216, every 250 ms.
  3. 3Open both SPI devices and set 1 MHz. The right eye is optional.
  4. 4Claim DC and RESET as outputs. If the stock app holds them, retry every 0.5 s and say who does.
  5. 5Pulse reset low for 10 ms.
  6. 6Send the init sequence, then 0xAF, then clear the panel.
  7. 7Animate 12 frames, ping-pong, writing only the pages that changed.

Taking over safely

Safe order
toadi_eyes --takeover 8
# starts feeders, then runs
# /etc/init.d/toadi stop
Check who holds the eyes
toadi_eyes --check
spidev0.0  open by ToadiApp
gpio2      "LibgpiodDigitalOutput"
gpio216    TOGGLING
Do not Ctrl-C casually. With the feeders gone and nobody to take over, the robot reboots in about a second.
